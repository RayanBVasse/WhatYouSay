How labels are produced

Deterministic lexical signals (Level A)

Emotion and moral indicators are first computed with transparent lexicon-based scoring on your uploaded text. Emotion signals use the NRC framework, then normalize frequencies across your messages. Moral framing is estimated with weighted value-loading signals.

Context-aware narrative synthesis (Level B)

The model does not label one sentence in isolation. It uses broader conversational context plus Level A metrics to generate an interpretive summary of patterns across the exchange.

Interpretation limits and privacy

Short excerpts shown here are illustrative examples, not full evidence. A line may appear positive or negative out of context; interpretation depends on surrounding messages and flow. For privacy, only short excerpts are displayed. Because this is your uploaded text, you may review your local source export for full context.

What this is not

This system reports communication patterns. It is not a clinical, psychiatric, or diagnostic tool.

Outputs are descriptive summaries based on statistical patterning. They are not evidence, assessments, or evaluations of individuals. They should not be used in legal, clinical, employment, or interpersonal dispute contexts.

What these patterns mean

Mode and role patterns are derived from how your messages function in the conversation, not from their content or tone.

Mode: assertive means your messages tend to take a clear position, introduce information, or push a direction rather than hedging or deferring.

Role: responder means a significant share of your messages follow someone else's; you engage with what's already on the table rather than opening new threads.

Role: challenger means your messages frequently introduce a correction, counterpoint, or clarification to what was said before.

These two role patterns often appear together. Responding and challenging are not opposites. You can engage primarily in response to others while consistently pushing back on what they say. That combination often signals someone who is attentive and engaged but not passive.

These are patterns in this conversation, not fixed characteristics.
